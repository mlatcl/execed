#### Executive learning outcomes (draft)

Participants should be able to:

1. **Frame AI correctly for leadership**: distinguish “intelligent systems” from “intelligent entities”, and translate AI capability into changes in organisational decision-making.
2. **Identify where value really comes from**: recognise how information flow, coordination, and scaled micro-decisions (e.g. supply chains) create advantage or fragility.
3. **Define intent and incentives**: specify what a system is optimising for, detect misalignment with organisational intent, and anticipate gaming/adversarial behaviour.
4. **Reason explicitly under uncertainty**: use ranges, scenarios, and decision thresholds; separate facts, estimates, and judgment; design “pause when unsure”.
5. **Design for operational reality**: apply “mission control” principles (roles, escalation, simulation, handoffs) to high-stakes socio-technical systems.
6. **Govern trust and accountability**: decide what can be delegated, to whom/what, under what oversight; ensure auditability, ownership, and recourse.
7. **Recognise and mitigate manipulation risks**: understand “System Zero” dynamics (influence below awareness), data asymmetry, and the risks of algorithmic homogeneity.
8. **Build human capability as the advantage**: treat skills, routines, decision rights, and culture as the core platform for AI value (augmentation-first).
9. **Lead the human–agent shift**: understand how LLMs/coding agents change work, where they fail, and how to measure decision quality and prevent automation complacency.
10. **Prioritise resilience**: favour robustness, recovery, and guardrails over brittle optimisation; treat downside (risk) as part of ROI.
