## Chapter contributions (draft, exec-facing)

> Theme labels refer to `backlog/documentation/2026-01-26_execed-learning-outcomes.md`:
> A Framing, B Information topography, C Intent/incentives, D Uncertainty, E Ops/mission control, F Trust/accountability, G System Zero/manipulation, H Human capability + agents, I Robustness/resilience.

### Prologue
Sets the modern “AI moment” as a branding/investment shift and reframes leadership questions around **which decisions are being automated** and **who bears downside when systems are wrong**. *(Themes: A, C, F)*

### Chapter 1: Gods and Robots
Introduces the “atomic human” lens: humans are bandwidth-limited and succeed through culture and coordination, while machines scale through fast communication—so business risk/value comes from **scaled decision systems**, not robot-like entities. *(Themes: A, B, H)*

### Chapter 2: Automatons
Explains how large organisations operate via **information topography** and why automation works only in information-complete contexts; decomposition scales throughput but can hide systemic failure. *(Themes: B, E, I)*

### Chapter 3: Intent
Shows why intent is hard to encode: expertise is choosing context, and systems optimise for what’s measured—creating predictable failure modes (gaming, adversaries, misalignment) and “intellectual debt”. *(Themes: C, B, I)*

### Chapter 4: Persistence
Uses evolution as a counter-model: nature optimises for “don’t fail” and robustness, while engineered systems often overfit narrow objectives—so leaders should prioritise guardrails, recovery, and resilience. *(Themes: I, D)*

### Chapter 5: Enlightenment
Places AI in a longer arc of information revolutions and reductionist science: model+data+compute yields prediction, but control systems and feedback often beat grand theories in messy reality. *(Themes: A, I, B)*

### Chapter 6: The Gremlin of Uncertainty
Makes uncertainty central: confidence must be earned and communicated; probability is a management tool; good teams combine planning with improvisation and delayed commitment. *(Themes: D, E)*

### Chapter 7: It’s Not Rocket Science or Brain Surgery
Translates high-stakes performance into organisational design: “mission control” thinking (roles, escalation, simulation, interfaces) is how reliability is achieved—systems, not heroes. *(Themes: E, H)*

### Chapter 8: System Zero
Describes how platforms manipulate below awareness at scale, how homogeneity creates systemic risk, and why data rights/incentives are safety infrastructure, not compliance overhead. *(Themes: G, C, F)*

### Chapter 9: Design for a Brain
Explains the mismatch between machine pattern-matching and human understanding; warns against “human-like” narratives and stresses designing for real failure modes and social context. *(Themes: A, H, D)*

### Chapter 10: Gaslighting
Shows how dominant paradigms and surveillance can distort reality and decision-making; argues for pluralism (independent review, red teaming) to avoid organisational “model blinkers”. *(Themes: G, C)*

### Chapter 11: Human–Analogue Machines
Connects modern AI’s power (data+compute) with its fragility (confident errors, “voids”, adversarial inputs) and the need for pause/escalation instead of outsourcing judgement. *(Themes: D, F, H)*

### Chapter 12: Trust
Frames trust as an executive design obligation: accountability must remain human; delegation requires oversight, auditability, and stewardship; power follows data asymmetry and incentives. *(Themes: F, C, G)*

### Epilogue
Re-centres the problem as institutional: without strong governance, the tech+incentive combination produces information totalitarianism; success is resilience, education, and plural institutions. *(Themes: F, G, I)*
