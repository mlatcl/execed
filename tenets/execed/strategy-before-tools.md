# Tenet: Strategy before tools

## Tenet

**Title**: Strategy before tools

**Description**: We treat AI and data as *means*, not ends. We start by clarifying the strategic choice (growth, cost, risk, resilience, differentiation) and the decision we want to improve, then work backward to what information, capability, and governance are required. This keeps the course grounded in executive decision-making rather than tool-chasing.

**Quote**: *"If it doesn’t change a decision, it isn’t strategy."*

**Examples**:
- Before discussing models, define the executive decision (e.g., “which segments get expanded service capacity next quarter?”) and what “better” means.
- Use a “decision inventory”: list recurring decisions, frequency, risk, and current evidence; pick the one with the biggest strategic leverage.
- Frame initiatives as options: what choice does this create, and what constraints does it remove?

**Counter-examples**:
- Starting with a vendor demo and then searching for a “use case” to justify it.
- Measuring success by model accuracy alone when the decision, incentives, and operating model remain unchanged.
- Treating “AI adoption” as a goal without a defined strategic outcome.

**Conflicts**:
- Potential conflict with **move-fast-with-guardrails** when time pressure pushes “just ship something”.
- Resolution: Use a minimal “strategy check” (decision, owner, value hypothesis, risk) as the entry ticket to any pilot.

## Alignment with *The Atomic Human* takeaways

- **Prologue + Chapter 2–3**: hype and tooling are distractions; the real question is what decision is being automated and who bears the downside.
- **Chapter 8 (System Zero)**: incentives dominate outcomes; a system optimises what you reward, not what you intend.
