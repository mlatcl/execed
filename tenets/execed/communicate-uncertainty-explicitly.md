# Tenet: Communicate uncertainty explicitly

## Tenet

**Title**: Communicate uncertainty explicitly

**Description**: Executives make decisions under uncertainty; pretending otherwise creates brittle plans and mistrust. We state assumptions, confidence, limits, and failure modes in plain language. This improves decision quality, enables accountability, and makes it easier to revise course when reality differs from forecasts.

**Quote**: *"Clarity about uncertainty is a leadership skill."*

**Examples**:
- Present ranges and scenarios, not single-point forecasts; document the assumptions that matter most.
- Include “how this could fail” in proposals and specify leading signals that indicate trouble early.
- Separate facts, estimates, and judgment explicitly in board/exco materials.

**Counter-examples**:
- Overconfident predictions that suppress dissent and reduce learning.
- Hiding uncertainty in complex dashboards or technical jargon.
- Treating uncertainty as weakness rather than information.

**Conflicts**:
- Can conflict with a desire for decisiveness (“just give me the number”).
- Resolution: Pair uncertainty with clear decision options and recommended action, including what to monitor and when to revisit.
